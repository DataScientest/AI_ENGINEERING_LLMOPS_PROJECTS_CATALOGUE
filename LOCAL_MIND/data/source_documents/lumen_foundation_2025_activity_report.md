# 2025 Annual Report — Lumen Foundation

## Foreword

2025 was a turning point for the Lumen Foundation. Founded in 2012 to promote access to digital education in rural areas, the foundation expanded its work to three new regions, doubled the number of beneficiaries of its programs and began a deep transformation of its internal organization. This report presents the results for the year, the difficulties encountered and the directions chosen for 2026.

## 1. Highlights of the year

The flagship program "Connection for All" equipped 214 schools, compared with 96 in 2024. This acceleration results from the partnership signed in February with the regional consortium NumOuest, which provided 1.8 million euros of funding over three years and privileged access to fiber-optic infrastructure in sparsely populated areas.

The second highlight is the September launch of the "Passerelle" program, aimed at adults retraining professionally. In four months, 1,340 people enrolled in the training pathways, against an initial target of 800. This success has nevertheless put the teaching teams under strain: the staffing ratio went from 1 trainer for 15 learners to 1 for 24, a level considered unsustainable beyond the first quarter of 2026.

Finally, the foundation moved its headquarters from Nantes to a shared site with two other social and solidarity economy organizations, reducing its operating costs by 22%.

## 2. Program results

### 2.1 Connection for All

The program benefited approximately 38,000 students. Evaluations carried out with the EduMetrics laboratory show an average improvement of 14 points in basic digital skills in schools equipped for more than a year. However, three departments account for deployment delays linked to difficulties in recruiting local technicians; 31 schools planned for 2025 have been postponed to 2026.

### 2.2 Passerelle

Of the 1,340 enrollees, 61% completed their pathway on time, 24% are still in progress and 15% dropped out. Dropouts are concentrated among the groups furthest from employment, which led the foundation to trial enhanced mentoring at two pilot sites starting in November. The first results, still partial, show the dropout rate halved at these sites.

### 2.3 Seniors workshops

The long-standing program of digital workshops for seniors reached 4,900 participants, a stable figure. The executive committee decided to maintain it as is in 2026, while studying its gradual transfer to local partner associations by 2027.

## 3. Financial situation

Revenue for the year amounts to 6.4 million euros, up 31% compared with 2024. This growth comes mainly from the NumOuest partnership and an exceptional bequest of 600,000 euros. Expenses reach 5.9 million euros, of which 68% are directly allocated to programs. The surplus of 500,000 euros will be allocated to the reserve fund, bringing it to the equivalent of five months of operations.

The statutory auditor certified the accounts without qualification. One point of vigilance is flagged: dependence on NumOuest funding, which now represents 28% of revenue. The board of directors has asked that no funder exceed 25% of revenue from 2027.

## 4. Organization and teams

Staff headcount rose from 42 to 57 people. This level of growth revealed weaknesses: longer recruitment times, an insufficient onboarding process, and lasting overload of the support team. A human resources department was created in October, and an internal restructuring plan with six workstreams was approved for 2026 (recruitment, onboarding, internal training, collaboration tools, data management, quality of working life).

The volunteer network counts 610 active members. The foundation introduced an engagement charter and a mandatory initial training course of two half-days, completed by 480 volunteers in the first year.

## 5. Identified difficulties and risks

Three main risks were entered in the risk register. The first concerns the sustainability of the Passerelle program: without recruiting additional trainers, teaching quality will deteriorate from the second quarter of 2026. The second concerns financial dependence on the NumOuest consortium. The third concerns compliance with the data protection regulation: the October internal audit found undocumented retention periods on two business applications, with a compliance plan expected by March 2026.

## 6. 2026 directions

The board of directors set four priorities for 2026: consolidate Passerelle by recruiting eight trainers and generalizing enhanced mentoring; complete the postponed rollouts of Connection for All; diversify funding with a target of three new major partners; and carry out the internal restructuring plan, in particular compliance on data protection.

The foundation also sets itself a cross-cutting objective: to systematically measure the impact of its programs with indicators published annually, in order to report on its work to its funders and the general public.
