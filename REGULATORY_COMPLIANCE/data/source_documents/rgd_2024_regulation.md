# Data Governance Regulation 2024 (RGD-2024)

**Information Systems Oversight Authority (ACSI)**

*Entry into force: January 1, 2024*

---

## Article 1 — Scope

This regulation applies to any entity, public or private, that processes personal data relating to more than 500 natural persons.

**Definitions:**
- "Personal data": any information relating to an identified or identifiable natural person
- "Processing": any operation on personal data such as collection, storage, modification, disclosure

---

## Article 12 — Register of processing activities

### 12.1 — Obligation to maintain

Every entity subject to this regulation must maintain a register of the personal data processing activities it carries out.

This register must contain:
- The purposes of the processing
- The categories of data processed
- The recipients or categories of recipients
- The planned retention periods
- A general description of the technical and organizational measures

### 12.2 — Update

The register must be updated at least **every six months**, and immediately in the event of a substantial change to the processing.

**Penalty**: The absence of an up-to-date register, or an update more than 18 months old, constitutes a major non-conformity (NC-001).

---

## Article 15 — Retention periods

### 15.1 — General principles

Personal data may not be retained beyond the period necessary for the purposes for which it was collected.

### 15.2 — Maximum periods by category

| Data category | Maximum period |
|---------------------|---------------|
| Customer data | 5 years after the end of the contractual relationship |
| Employee data | 5 years after the end of the employment contract |
| Accounting data | 10 years |
| Access logs | 1 year |

### 15.3 — Exception

Longer periods may be set by a reasoned decision of the entity, in particular for legal retention obligations.

**Penalty**: Retention beyond the established periods without justification constitutes a non-conformity (NC-007).

---

## Article 18 — Data Protection Officer

### 18.1 — Obligation to appoint

Every entity processing data relating to more than **500 people** must appoint a Data Protection Officer (DPO).

### 18.2 — Required skills

The DPO must have sufficient knowledge of personal data protection. They may be an employee of the entity or an external provider.

### 18.3 — Independence

The DPO may not receive instructions regarding the performance of their duties. They report directly to management.

**Penalty**: Failure to appoint a DPO when the threshold is exceeded constitutes a critical non-conformity (NC-003).

---

## Article 22 — Consent for sensitive data

### 22.1 — Definition

The following are considered sensitive data:
- Health data
- Biometric data
- Data relating to criminal convictions
- Data concerning ethnic origin, political, philosophical or religious opinions

### 22.2 — Explicit consent requirement

Processing of sensitive data requires the **explicit consent** of the data subject, obtained in a clear and unambiguous manner.

### 22.3 — Proof of consent

Proof of consent must be kept by the entity. Consent may be withdrawn at any time.

**Penalty**: Processing sensitive data without explicit consent constitutes a critical non-conformity (NC-018).

---

## Article 28 — Penalties

### 28.1 — Graduation of penalties

In case of non-conformity, the ACSI may impose:
1. A warning
2. A formal notice to comply
3. An administrative fine

### 28.2 — Fine amounts

For breaches of this regulation:
- LOW non-conformity: up to €50,000
- MEDIUM non-conformity: up to €500,000
- HIGH non-conformity: up to €1,000,000
- CRITICAL non-conformity: up to **2% of annual turnover**

### 28.3 — Aggravating circumstances

The following are aggravating circumstances:
- Repeat offense
- Failure to comply with a prior formal notice
- Number of persons concerned greater than 10,000

---

## Final provisions

This regulation enters into force on January 1, 2024.

Entities have a period of **6 months** to bring their existing processing into compliance.
