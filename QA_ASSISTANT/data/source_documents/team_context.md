# Team context

## The product and the team

TaxiFare predicts the fare of a New York taxi trip before the trip starts. The platform has a prediction API, a training pipeline that validates data and detects drift, and a monitoring stack with alerts. Its code is in the repository `DataScientest/AI_ENGINEERING_QA_ASSISTANT_TAXI_PLATFORM` (tag `v1.0`); its expected behaviour is in `docs/FUNCTIONAL_SPEC.md` of that repository.

The team has seven people: six developers and one QA engineer. Since the developers started using AI assistants to write code, they deliver about twice as many changes, and the QA engineer, the only person who designs tests and maintains the suite, has become the bottleneck.

## How a change moves today

1. A ticket with acceptance criteria is written in the tracker.
2. A developer implements it and opens a pull request. The CI runs the existing tests.
3. The QA engineer designs the test cases (nominal, limits, errors), then automates the ones worth keeping, at the right level: unit, integration or end-to-end.
4. The change is merged when the suite is green and the QA engineer agrees.

The suite has not kept up: 10 unit tests, 2 end-to-end tests, and 16% branch coverage of the services. Several modules have no test at all. Recent incidents were found in production, not by the suite.

## Constraints

- No additional QA hire is planned.
- Nothing generated by a tool is merged without a human review.
- The existing fixtures must be reused rather than rewritten.
- End-to-end tests need the full stack (about fifteen containers). They are slow and expensive; they should be kept for what lower levels cannot check.
- Tickets are written by several people and are not always precise.
